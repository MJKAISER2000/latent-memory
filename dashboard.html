Physics-Conforming Latent Twins → LLM Context Memory

Research dashboard · August 2026 · based on Chung, Bu & Verma, Physics-Conforming Latent Twins (arXiv:2606.15053) · RTX 3060 Laptop 6 GB

1 · The idea

The paper learns a surrogate solution operator for a physical system as encode → evolve in latent space → decode, with the latent generator constrained so that selected physical quantities are conserved or dissipated by construction:

$$\Psi(t,s,u_s) = d\big(m_{s\to t}(e(u_s))\big),\qquad m_{s\to t}(z)=e^{(t-s)W}z$$

Our transplant: treat LLM context as the driven physical system. State = a small latent memory \(z\in\mathbb R^{n_z}\); time = block index; and the generator is built so a designated subspace is exactly conserved while everything orthogonal decays monotonically:

$$P = I - CC^{\top},\qquad K = P\,(A-A^{\top})\,P,\qquad S = -(PB)(PB)^{\top}-\varepsilon P$$ $$W = K+S \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \underbrace{C^{\top}W = 0}_{\text{protected subspace}} \quad\text{and}\quad \underbrace{W+W^{\top}\preceq 0}_{\text{everything else decays}}$$

The paper's flow is autonomous; context memory is driven by incoming token blocks \(g_k = \mathrm{Enc}(x_k)\). Under forcing, exact conservation upgrades to an exact balance law — the ledger changes only by what came in the door:

$$z_{k+1}=e^{\Delta t\,W}z_k+g_k \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; C^{\top}z_t \;=\; C^{\top}z_s+\sum_{k=s}^{t-1}C^{\top}g_k \qquad\textit{(verified to } 3.2\times10^{-13}\textit{, no loss term)}$$

Conservation is then free; the entire learning problem is making the flux \(C^{\top}g_k\) mean the right thing — the input-side analogue of the paper's pullback-matching loss (their Eq. 7), whose Fig. 10 shows conservation without anchoring is conservation of nothing.

3 · Experiment timeline — findings and failures

Structural self-test VERIFIED

All guarantees hold at machine precision (float64): \(\max|C^\top W| = 5\times10^{-16}\), \(\lambda_{\max}(W{+}W^\top)\le 0\), ledger drift \(2\times10^{-11}\) at \(t{=}10^4\), driven balance law exact to \(3.2\times10^{-13}\) over 128 forced steps. The machinery does exactly what it claims. This part never wavered.

L0 — synthetic ledger ARTIFACT

Train a running-total task at \(K{=}64\), test at \(K{=}1024\). Headline looked spectacular: pinned 12.7× better than the scalar-decay baseline at 16× extrapolation, error falling as \(1/\sqrt t\). It did not survive its own audit — see the failure ledger. What genuinely survives: pinned lands \(\sim\)1.55× off the information-theoretic floor and tracks the sample-specific total with \(r=0.987\). A real working accumulator — just not evidence of superiority.

L0.5 — anchor transport FALSIFIED

Hypothesis: supervising the ledger at one horizon (\(t{=}64\)) should give the pinned model every other horizon free, via the balance law. Result: sparse ≈ dense for every mode (transport ratio ≈ 1.0 across 18 configs). The transport comes from the shared per-block encoder, not from conservation. Killed one of our two claimed novelties. Cheap to learn, good to know.

Degeneracy self-check METHOD FIX

With a fixed deposit rate, \(M_t\) concentrates: \(\mathrm{SD}[M_t]/\mathbb E[M_t] = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[a]/t}\,/\,\mathbb E[a]\to 0\), so a no-input constant predictor scores 7% at \(t{=}1024\) — better than two of our three baselines. Fixed with per-sample rate jitter (spread pinned at ∼40% at every horizon, constant predictor degraded to 47%). Also added the correlation/skill diagnostics that MSE can't provide.

Adversarial audit (4 independent lenses) DECISIVE NEGATIVE

One-variable control: set \(\varepsilon{=}0\) in the dissipative baseline. Its error at \(t{=}1024\): 0.339 → 0.055. After a single scale+offset: 0.0111 vs pinned's 0.0112, identical correlation. Advantage = 1.00×. The entire L0 gap was our own hard-coded leak — see §5. This is the most valuable result so far: a real, mechanistic, reproducible explanation of a seductive false positive.

L0-Frontier — the corrected experiment FINDING (seed 0; replication running)

Under capacity pressure, where forgetting is necessary but ledger retention forbids it on one direction, and with every audit fix applied: gradient descent does not discover selective protection even when the parameterization can represent it. Learned decay parks in the train-horizon flat basin (\(d \approx 7{-}8\times10^{-4}\): invisible over 64 steps, fatal over 1024). The soft \(K{+}S\) mode finds the right direction (align 0.98) but its residual leak halves its skill. The hard \(C^\top W = 0\) constraint alone wins both axes — ledger skill +1.00 and best content among protecting modes — while forgetting aggressively everywhere else. The surviving claim is about optimization, not expressivity: hard structure substitutes for gradient pressure that short-horizon training cannot provide.

The gradient across modes is the finding. Learned decay parks in a flat basin: any rate below \(\sim 1/K_{\text{train}}\) is invisible during training (\(e^{-7.8\times10^{-4}\cdot 64}\!\approx\!0.95\)) yet fatal at test (\(e^{-7.8\times10^{-4}\cdot 1024}\!\approx\!0.45\)); SGD gets no gradient signal to push it to zero. diag could represent the pinned solution and never found it. ks found the direction (0.98 aligned) but its residual leak still cost half its skill, and it paid on content. The hard constraint delivers exactly what optimization won't: zero decay on the protected direction, aggressive forgetting (median decay 4.1) everywhere else — winning both axes.

Scope honestly: one seed so far (two more in flight); a synthetic accumulation task, chosen because accumulation is the one thing a conserved ledger is for — the L1 question is whether real-LM `count`-style tasks inherit the effect. Also note a real design issue surfaced: the unbounded accumulator direction drifts out of the decoder's training range at long horizon (all protecting modes show content-late degradation), suggesting bounded ledger encodings (means, not sums) for L1.

5 · Failure ledger — the ε artifact, in full

The original code applied the decay floor asymmetrically:

$$W_{\text{dissip}} = -BB^{\top}-\varepsilon I \qquad\text{vs}\qquad W_{\text{pinned}} = P(A{-}A^{\top})P-(PB)(PB)^{\top}-\varepsilon P$$

The \(\varepsilon I\) term forces every direction of the baseline to leak at rate \(\varepsilon\); the \(\varepsilon P\) term exempts the protected direction. With \(\varepsilon = 10^{-3}\) and \(K_{\text{test}} = 1024\):

$$\varepsilon\,K_{\text{test}} = 1.024 \approx 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \text{readout gain} = e^{-\varepsilon t}\Big|_{t=1024} \approx 0.36$$

The forced leak timescale equalled the test horizon — the baseline was scripted to fail at exactly the horizon we measured. The auditor reproduced the entire dissipative error curve with zero free parameters as quadrature of (per-block estimation noise) and (\(\varepsilon\)-leak bias), and the pinned/dissipative ratio tracks \(\varepsilon t\), not extrapolation depth: 1.0× at \(\varepsilon t{=}0.06\) → 12.8× as \(\varepsilon t\) crosses 1.

Also wrong, for the record: (1) pinned had 2× the live generator parameters (both \(A\) and \(B\) alive; one of them dead in each baseline) despite a claimed "identical budget"; (2) the readout had no gain/bias, so a deterministic, knowable miscalibration was scored as error; (3) the task label \(M_t=\sum a_k\) was literally the pinned mode's built-in invariant; (4) init_spectral=0.03 put the conservative mode's rotation period (∼154 steps) between train and test horizons — its failure was invisible in training, maximal at test; (5) my "\(1/\sqrt t\) prediction confirmed to three digits" was a property of the metric, and the three-digit match was seed luck; (6) "Mamba/IndexMem corner" mislabeled the baseline — their decay is learned with no floor.
Why this failure is worth keeping: it is a precise, reproducible case study in how structure-preservation results flatter themselves — hard-constrain the property your metric measures, exempt your method from a leak the baseline is forced to carry, and extrapolation plots will look magnificent. The audit protocol (one-variable controls, oracle recalibration, no-input floors, Bayes-floor comparison) transfers to any structured-memory evaluation, including published ones.

6 · What survives, honestly

  • The construction works as specified. Exact subspace conservation under forcing, machine precision, verified. An auditable memory — the balance law \(C^\top z_t = C^\top z_s + \sum C^\top g_k\) is checkable online, and its defect is a principled staleness detector.
  • A validated audit methodology for structured-memory claims (arguably the most transferable artifact of the project so far).
  • The frontier finding (seed 0, replication in flight): learned decay can represent selective protection but SGD does not find it under train-short/test-long — the flat basin below \(d\sim 1/K_{\text{train}}\) gives no gradient signal. The hard constraint buys optimization reliability, not expressivity. If it replicates, this is the defensible core claim, and it makes a concrete prediction for real systems: IndexMem-style learned-λ memories should under-retain at horizons beyond their training length, fixably by pinning.
  • The L1/L2 assets are built and unblocked: haystack corpus with per-block durability labels (retrieve / update / count — count's answer appears nowhere in the context), frozen-LM block-state precompute, SLURM scripts for the university cluster.

Prior-art position: the \(K{+}S\) decomposition matches Lipschitz RNN (ICLR 2021); scalar/diag decay is the Mamba/S4 corner; skew is the unitary-RNN corner. The closest applied system, IndexMem (2026), uses learned scalar decay with no protected subspace. The pinned construction + balance-law framing for LLM memory remains unclaimed territory as far as we found — but it must first be shown to beat matched learned baselines, which it has not yet been.

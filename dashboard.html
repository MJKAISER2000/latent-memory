1 · The idea

The paper learns a surrogate solution operator for a physical system as encode → evolve in latent space → decode, with the latent generator constrained so that selected physical quantities are conserved or dissipated by construction:

$$\Psi(t,s,u_s) = d\big(m_{s\to t}(e(u_s))\big),\qquad m_{s\to t}(z)=e^{(t-s)W}z$$

Our transplant: treat LLM context as the driven physical system. State = a small latent memory \(z\in\mathbb R^{n_z}\); time = block index; and the generator is built so a designated subspace is exactly conserved while everything orthogonal decays monotonically:

$$P = I - CC^{\top},\qquad K = P\,(A-A^{\top})\,P,\qquad S = -(PB)(PB)^{\top}-\varepsilon P$$ $$W = K+S \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \underbrace{C^{\top}W = 0}_{\text{protected subspace}} \quad\text{and}\quad \underbrace{W+W^{\top}\preceq 0}_{\text{everything else decays}}$$

The paper's flow is autonomous; context memory is driven by incoming token blocks \(g_k = \mathrm{Enc}(x_k)\). Under forcing, exact conservation upgrades to an exact balance law — the ledger changes only by what came in the door:

$$z_{k+1}=e^{\Delta t\,W}z_k+g_k \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; C^{\top}z_t \;=\; C^{\top}z_s+\sum_{k=s}^{t-1}C^{\top}g_k \qquad\textit{(verified to } 3.2\times10^{-13}\textit{, no loss term)}$$

Conservation is then free; the entire learning problem is making the flux \(C^{\top}g_k\) mean the right thing — the input-side analogue of the paper's pullback-matching loss (their Eq. 7), whose Fig. 10 shows conservation without anchoring is conservation of nothing.

3 · Experiment timeline — findings and failures

Structural self-test VERIFIED

All guarantees hold at machine precision (float64): \(\max|C^\top W| = 5\times10^{-16}\), \(\lambda_{\max}(W{+}W^\top)\le 0\), ledger drift \(2\times10^{-11}\) at \(t{=}10^4\), driven balance law exact to \(3.2\times10^{-13}\) over 128 forced steps. The machinery does exactly what it claims. This part never wavered.

L0 — synthetic ledger ARTIFACT

Train a running-total task at \(K{=}64\), test at \(K{=}1024\). Headline looked spectacular: pinned 12.7× better than the scalar-decay baseline at 16× extrapolation, error falling as \(1/\sqrt t\). It did not survive its own audit — see the failure ledger. What genuinely survives: pinned lands \(\sim\)1.55× off the information-theoretic floor and tracks the sample-specific total with \(r=0.987\). A real working accumulator — just not evidence of superiority.

L0.5 — anchor transport FALSIFIED

Hypothesis: supervising the ledger at one horizon (\(t{=}64\)) should give the pinned model every other horizon free, via the balance law. Result: sparse ≈ dense for every mode (transport ratio ≈ 1.0 across 18 configs). The transport comes from the shared per-block encoder, not from conservation. Killed one of our two claimed novelties. Cheap to learn, good to know.

Degeneracy self-check METHOD FIX

With a fixed deposit rate, \(M_t\) concentrates: \(\mathrm{SD}[M_t]/\mathbb E[M_t] = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[a]/t}\,/\,\mathbb E[a]\to 0\), so a no-input constant predictor scores 7% at \(t{=}1024\) — better than two of our three baselines. Fixed with per-sample rate jitter (spread pinned at ∼40% at every horizon, constant predictor degraded to 47%). Also added the correlation/skill diagnostics that MSE can't provide.

Adversarial audit (4 independent lenses) DECISIVE NEGATIVE

One-variable control: set \(\varepsilon{=}0\) in the dissipative baseline. Its error at \(t{=}1024\): 0.339 → 0.055. After a single scale+offset: 0.0111 vs pinned's 0.0112, identical correlation. Advantage = 1.00×. The entire L0 gap was our own hard-coded leak — see §5. This is the most valuable result so far: a real, mechanistic, reproducible explanation of a seductive false positive.

L0-Frontier — the corrected experiment REPLICATED 3/3 SEEDS

Under capacity pressure, where forgetting is necessary but ledger retention forbids it on one direction, and with every audit fix applied: gradient descent does not deliver selective protection even when the parameterization can represent it. Learned decay converges to \(d^\*\approx 7{-}9\times10^{-4}\) — the SNR probe showed this is an attractor of the short-horizon objective (mild forgetting cuts interference; the ledger cost over 64 steps is absorbed by the learned gain), and that same optimum is fatal at 1024 steps (gain \(\approx 0.45\)). The soft \(K{+}S\) mode finds the right direction (align 0.89–0.98, 3/3 seeds) but keeps the leak. The hard \(C^\top W = 0\) constraint alone wins both axes — ledger skill +1.000 and best content among protecting modes — while forgetting aggressively everywhere else. The claim: training optimizes retention for the horizon it sees and trades away everything longer; hard structure removes the protected direction from that trade entirely.

5 · Failure ledger — the ε artifact, in full

The original code applied the decay floor asymmetrically:

$$W_{\text{dissip}} = -BB^{\top}-\varepsilon I \qquad\text{vs}\qquad W_{\text{pinned}} = P(A{-}A^{\top})P-(PB)(PB)^{\top}-\varepsilon P$$

The \(\varepsilon I\) term forces every direction of the baseline to leak at rate \(\varepsilon\); the \(\varepsilon P\) term exempts the protected direction. With \(\varepsilon = 10^{-3}\) and \(K_{\text{test}} = 1024\):

$$\varepsilon\,K_{\text{test}} = 1.024 \approx 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \text{readout gain} = e^{-\varepsilon t}\Big|_{t=1024} \approx 0.36$$

The forced leak timescale equalled the test horizon — the baseline was scripted to fail at exactly the horizon we measured. The auditor reproduced the entire dissipative error curve with zero free parameters as quadrature of (per-block estimation noise) and (\(\varepsilon\)-leak bias), and the pinned/dissipative ratio tracks \(\varepsilon t\), not extrapolation depth: 1.0× at \(\varepsilon t{=}0.06\) → 12.8× as \(\varepsilon t\) crosses 1.

Also wrong, for the record: (1) pinned had 2× the live generator parameters (both \(A\) and \(B\) alive; one of them dead in each baseline) despite a claimed "identical budget"; (2) the readout had no gain/bias, so a deterministic, knowable miscalibration was scored as error; (3) the task label \(M_t=\sum a_k\) was literally the pinned mode's built-in invariant; (4) init_spectral=0.03 put the conservative mode's rotation period (∼154 steps) between train and test horizons — its failure was invisible in training, maximal at test; (5) my "\(1/\sqrt t\) prediction confirmed to three digits" was a property of the metric, and the three-digit match was seed luck; (6) "Mamba/IndexMem corner" mislabeled the baseline — their decay is learned with no floor.
Why this failure is worth keeping: it is a precise, reproducible case study in how structure-preservation results flatter themselves — hard-constrain the property your metric measures, exempt your method from a leak the baseline is forced to carry, and extrapolation plots will look magnificent. The audit protocol (one-variable controls, oracle recalibration, no-input floors, Bayes-floor comparison) transfers to any structured-memory evaluation, including published ones.

6 · What survives, honestly

  • The construction works as specified. Exact subspace conservation under forcing, machine precision, verified. An auditable memory — the balance law \(C^\top z_t = C^\top z_s + \sum C^\top g_k\) is checkable online, and its defect is a principled staleness detector.
  • A validated audit methodology for structured-memory claims (arguably the most transferable artifact of the project so far).
  • The frontier finding (replicated 3/3 seeds, mechanism measured): learned decay can represent selective protection but training optimizes it away — the short-horizon objective has an interior optimum \(d^\*\!\approx\!7\times10^{-4}\) (SNR probe: gradient sign flips there; all 12 learned runs converged to it) that under-retains catastrophically at 16× the training horizon. The hard constraint removes the protected direction from that interference-vs-retention trade entirely. Concrete prediction for real systems: learned-λ memories (IndexMem-style) under-retain beyond their training length, fixably by pinning — and no initialization trick can save them, because the optimum is an attractor (init0 rescue arm testing this now).
  • The L1/L2 assets are built and unblocked: haystack corpus with per-block durability labels (retrieve / update / count — count's answer appears nowhere in the context), frozen-LM block-state precompute, SLURM scripts for the university cluster.

Prior-art position: the \(K{+}S\) decomposition matches Lipschitz RNN (ICLR 2021); scalar/diag decay is the Mamba/S4 corner; skew is the unitary-RNN corner. The closest applied system, IndexMem (2026), uses learned scalar decay with no protected subspace. The pinned construction + balance-law framing for LLM memory remains unclaimed territory as far as we found — but it must first be shown to beat matched learned baselines, which it has not yet been.

F1 · Hard conservation substitutes for optimization pressure that short-horizon training cannot provide REPLICATING

Claim
(Third revision — narrowed by our own rescue suite.) Learned decay at practitioner-standard initialization converges to a horizon-myopic attractor and under-retains catastrophically beyond the training horizon. Soft fixes fail; removal fixes work: explicit regularization toward zero decay loses the tug-of-war with the objective (skill −4.0 ± 2.5), while removing retention from the objective's reach — by hard projection (\(C^\top W{=}0\), exact + auditable) or by freezing decay at zero (softplus init0; the parameterization gradient vanishes there) — restores full skill. What we can not claim: that hard structure uniquely wins — frozen-zero scalar decay matched pinned on the ledger and beat it on content on this task, because the task turned out not to require forgetting at all (see Caveats).
Mechanism
Revised after the SNR probe refuted our first story. We initially blamed a flat basin (no gradient signal below \(d\sim 1/K_{\text{train}}\)). Direct measurement says otherwise: gradient SNR across minibatches is 8–11 at every decay value — Adam sees the parameter fine. The signs reveal the real mechanism: for \(d \lesssim 5\times10^{-4}\) the training gradient pushes decay up, above \(3\times10^{-3}\) it pushes decay down — the short-horizon objective has an interior optimum \(d^\*\!\approx\! 7\times10^{-4}\) (mild forgetting cuts interference over 64 steps; the ledger cost \(e^{-d^\*K_{\text{train}}}\!\approx\!0.95\) is absorbed by the learned gain). Every learned mode converged to exactly this attractor (6.7–9.0×10-4, all seeds). At \(K_{\text{test}}=16K_{\text{train}}\), that same optimum gives gain \(e^{-d^\*K_{\text{test}}}\!\approx\!0.45\) — catastrophic. Training doesn't fail to find protection; it actively trades it away at the horizon it can see. Consequence: no initialization can rescue the baselines (an attractor recaptures init-at-zero — prediction under test in the rescue suite). Only changing the objective (train at deployment length — expensive) or removing the ledger direction from the tradeoff structurally (\(C^\top W{=}0\)) escapes.
Evidence
Frontier experiment, matched learned baselines, all audit fixes, 3/3 seeds: scalar skill −6.54±0.21; diag −6.12±0.18 (0/3 seeds zero a direction); soft K+S finds the direction every seed (align 0.89–0.98) but keeps a \(\sim\!7{\times}10^{-4}\) leak, skill +0.25±0.17; pinned +1.000±0.000 with exact zero decay and the best, most stable content (0.62±0.10). Every learned decay parked at 6.7–9.0×10-4 from init 10-3. Landscape scan: in the sub-critical region \(dK_{\text{train}}\!<\!0.1\), \(L_{\text{train}}\) varies 6,365× less than \(L_{\text{test}}\); single-batch gradient 237× weaker than super-critical. SNR probe (done): refuted the flat-basin variant — SNR 8–11 at all decay values, gradient sign flips around \(d^\*\approx 7\times10^{-4}\), confirming an interior attractor of the training objective, which is where all 12 learned runs converged. Rescue suite (done, 2 seeds/arm):

Note my failed prediction, for the record: I predicted init0's decay would drift back up to the attractor. It did not — softplus's vanishing gradient at zero makes init0 a de-facto freeze, i.e. a parameterization-level removal fix.
Significance
It reframes what structure is for: not expressivity (the baselines could express the solution) but reliability of optimization under train-short/test-long — which is the universal regime of LLM memory (trained on 4–16k, deployed on 100k+). Concrete falsifiable prediction: learned-λ latent memories (e.g. IndexMem's \(M\!\leftarrow\!\lambda M + \eta\sum\!\text{Lin}(k)\otimes v^\top\)) under-retain beyond their training length, and pinning a supervised subspace fixes it without retraining tricks.
Caveats
The capacity-pressure premise failed empirically: the task was designed so never-forgetting would fail on content via interference — it doesn't (frozen-zero decay has the best content). So this task cannot distinguish "protect selectively" from "never decay anything", and pinned's selective-forgetting advantage is unsupported by current data — a task where forgetting is provably necessary is now T1 priority #1. Also: synthetic accumulation family only; n=3 (n=10 running); one scale (\(n_z{=}8\)); the constraint helps only quantities you can name and supervise a flux for. Pinned's remaining unique value: machine-exact conservation, the auditable balance law, and guarantees no init trick provides.

F2 · Our own headline result was an artifact — and the audit protocol that caught it transfers NEGATIVE + METHOD

Claim
The original "pinned is 12.7–29× better at 16× extrapolation" was manufactured by a fixed decay floor applied asymmetrically (\(-\varepsilon I\) for the baseline, \(-\varepsilon P\) for the method) with \(\varepsilon K_{\text{test}} = 1.024\) — the baseline was scripted to fail at exactly the measured horizon. One-variable control (\(\varepsilon{=}0\)): advantage 1.00×, identical correlation 0.9878.
Significance
The failure pattern generalizes: hard-constrain the property your metric measures, exempt your method from a leak the baseline carries, and extrapolation plots look magnificent. The audit checklist that caught it — one-variable controls, oracle affine recalibration on held-out data, no-input floor, Bayes-floor comparison, live-parameter counts, timescale collision checks (\(\varepsilon t\), rotation periods, \(1/K_{\text{train}}\)) — is directly applicable to published structured-memory results, most of which report none of these.

F3 · Anchor transport is encoder-borne, not conservation-borne FALSIFIED HYPOTHESIS

Claim
We predicted the balance law would let one supervised horizon pin all horizons ("structure converts sparse supervision into dense supervision"). False: sparse ≈ dense for every generator mode (interior errors 0.164/0.145/0.143 across modes; 18 configs, 3 seeds). The transport mechanism is the position-shared encoder: one summed constraint over many random samples identifies the per-block flux function regardless of generator structure.
Significance
Kills a seductive story we almost built on, and clarifies the division of labor: the encoder determines what a supervised functional means; the generator determines whether it survives. Useful design lens for any latent-memory system.

F4 · A driven memory with an exact, auditable balance law VERIFIED MACHINERY

Claim
Under forcing, the pinned construction upgrades conservation to \(C^\top z_t = C^\top z_s + \sum_k C^\top g_k\) — exact to \(3.2\times10^{-13}\) (float64) over driven rollouts, with no loss term. The memory's protected content changes only by explicit, logged fluxes.
Significance
An accounting property no attention window or learned-decay memory has: the ledger is auditable online, and the compatibility defect \(|\mathcal C(d(z)) - \mathcal C_Z(z)|\) doubles as a staleness/OOD alarm. This is the piece that transfers directly to the physics branch (a context-resident latent scratchpad whose invariants the LLM cannot corrupt by reasoning about them).
Caveats
Exactness is in latent space; meaning still depends on the learned flux (F3). Float32 drift at \(10^4\) steps is \(\sim\!10^{-2}\) — use float64 accumulators or renormalize if auditing at extreme horizons.

F5 · Scoped engineering observations OBSERVATION

  • Unbounded accumulators drift out of the decoder's range. Every protecting mode shows late-horizon content degradation because \(C^\top z\) grows past anything seen in training. Design consequence for L1: pin bounded encodings (means/rates, not raw sums), or condition the decoder on horizon.
  • Pure rotation is an initialization lottery. Skew-only generators put the readout on a rotation of period \(\sim\!2\pi/|\lambda|\); whether that survives to the test horizon is luck (observed spread 0.04–0.86 rel. err across seeds at fixed hyperparameters).
  • Norm preservation \(\ne\) functional preservation. Orthogonal/unitary transitions conserve \(\|z\|\) while freely rotating any particular readout into oblivion — pinning a named functional is a strictly different (and cheaper) constraint than unitarity.
  • torch.matrix_exp cost explodes as \(\|W\|\) grows during training (Padé scaling-and-squaring; observed 15× slowdowns). Bound the generator norm or use low-order integrators for the training loop.
